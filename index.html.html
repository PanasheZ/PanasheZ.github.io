About

Hi there, I am Panashe.

In the spirit of epistemic humility, I consider myself an exceptional lawyer and writer, and a plain average programmer. These skills naturally drove me to pursue a career in technology law and policy after two failed attempts at running startups and rejections from all but one of the computer science programmes I applied to. I have extensive experience working as a tech lawyer and policy analyst across various African jurisdictions.

Currently, the bulk of my work focuses on advancing African tech sovereignty and developing innovative legal, policy and, more recently, technical responses that seek to mitigate potential risks from present and future AI advances, as well as global catastrophic biological risks. I have a reasonably solid research track record, all things considered, with work appearing at venues such as the Centre for the Study of Existential Risk, the Cooperative AI Summer School, where I was invited to present a poster on exploratory work I am excited about and have linked below, and most recently the AI for Peace workshop at NeurIPS and the All Africa Intellectual Property Summit.

Research

My research follows six threads. Each has a landmark on the map above.

Law and AI

I am keen to explore effective, tractable, scalable and often neglected legal interventions that will help ensure that humanity navigates the transition to transformative AI safely and beneficially. Among my work in this domain, you will find work exploring the use of international law to pace the frontier. These days, I am interested in building upon O'Keefe et al.'s proposal on Law-Following AI and Maas and Olasunkanmi's proposal on Treaty-Following AI, which I believe are among the more compelling and concrete interventions for preventing and addressing AI-enabled power concentration, and in some exploratory technical work on legal alignment.

Cooperative AI, Multi-Agent Safety and Safe Pareto Improvements

The dynamics of AI interactions are rapidly changing in two important ways. Firstly, as more agentic systems are deployed, there is a shift from dyadic interactions (between a single model and a single user) to multi-agent interactions (potentially involving many different systems, agents and humans). Secondly, and as a result of the first change, the nature of these interactions changes from predominantly cooperative to mixed-motive.

I am keen on exploring governance and macrostrategy work related to ensuring safe and beneficial outcomes in these settings, particularly how populations of learning agents cooperate, compete, and coordinate, and what shapes the outcomes they reach.

I recently completed the Center on Long-Term Risk's Safe Pareto Improvements Fundamentals Program (September 2026), which gave me an appreciation for conceptual work on safe Pareto improvements, a robust mechanism for mitigating downsides from conflict between AIs. SPIs are a class of interventions on how agents negotiate that makes all parties better off, no matter how they would have negotiated without the SPI. I am continuing to work on conceptual questions and, most importantly, on neglected macrostrategy questions in the Center on Long-Term Risk's research agenda.

African Tech Sovereignty

I am a proud product of the African tech ecosystem, and I would like to see it grow, help address many of the continent's development challenges, and ensure that African states do not remain in a crippling state of technological dependency. To that end, an important aspect of my work is policy and governance research focused on advancing African technology sovereignty.

  • Book chapter
    AI-Generated Works and African Copyright Law: A Framework for Balancing Innovation and Intellectual Property Protection

    This research explores the implications of AI-generated works for traditional notions of authorship and ownership in Africa and beyond, drawing on recent judgments and scholarly opinion. It further assesses whether a balance can be struck between AI innovation and copyright law in a manner that brings about sustainable and inclusive growth.

Digital Minds

I have a keen interest in the legal, policy and macrostrategy questions surrounding AI moral patienthood, including AI legal personhood and enabling cooperation with AI through credible deals. I am particularly interested in exploring how extending legal personhood, economic rights, and certain protections to AI systems might facilitate positive-sum AI-human relations. In my view, long-term human safety will be inextricably linked to AI welfare. AI systems capable of contracting and holding property, for instance, have stronger incentives for cooperative behaviour and transparency about their priorities, potentially advancing alignment efforts. I have recently started publishing my thoughts on the subject.

Impact investing

Since high school I have been drawn to financial markets and their potential to make me a dollar out of fifteen cents, or, just as readily, to take me to zero. I am equally fascinated by investing in enterprises that generate measurable social or environmental impact, which has become something of a guiding principle. At present I have largely moved away from public markets. The bulk of the time and money I extend to this interest now goes to funding promising and ambitious early-stage impact-oriented startups as an angel investor, and to consulting for trusts and individuals keen on doing the same.
